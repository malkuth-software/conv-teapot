CPU renderer audit, 2026-11-12
host: Apple A18 Pro (2 performance and 4 efficiency cores); Apple clang; flags -O3 -ffp-contract=off
load averages while measured: about 6 (other work on the machine); every figure is the best of four
alternated runs of the build before (b6eabe8) and after, five frames each
view: the unified teapot, 2912 nodes, 960x600, default camera

What was changed (each gives the same picture, byte for byte, as the build before on one thread):
  1. The surface evaluation carries two numbers through the admission across the rows (value and
     d/du), not three: the rows' values do not depend on the parameter across, whose derivative
     comes from the basis alone.  tools/eval_bench: 374 -> 275 ns an evaluation, same sums.
  2. Predictions: the lattice's cells are filed once under bands of eight rows; bands are handed
     out a few at a time.  Before, each of 24 bands scanned every cell, and six threads took one
     chunk each, so an efficiency core set the time.
  3. Predictions: what does not change along a row or over a triangle is found before the pixel
     loop (corners as doubles, the row's differences, the edges' margins, one reciprocal).
  4. A trim's loop is tested against its box first.
  5. The capacity threshold returns its first form when the mass is under 4.5 times the smallest
     capacity (taken in over 90% of cells; no time gained by itself).

Found on the way: the band test allowed one row beyond a cell where a prediction can reach two, so
a few predictions were lost at each band's edge and the picture depended on the number of threads
(419 130 candidates on one thread, 419 104 on six). With two rows it does not.

Tried and withdrawn (no time gained, same pictures): skipping boxes wholly behind the tangent plane
a mirror or bake ray leaves, and testing each box of the per-ray tree once. The boxes such a ray
meets are the ones round its own origin, which straddle that plane.

Not done, with what was measured about each:
  - Edge samples: 7242 edge pixels cost 74 000 solves and 187 000 evaluations, as much as all the
    other pixels together (aa=0: 53 ms; aa=1: 69 ms before these changes).
  - The admission itself is most of an evaluation (compile_cell: about a quarter of a frame).
  - Mirror and bake rays walk the boxes round their own origin; on the GPU the gain came from
    knowing which cells are clear of their own part, which needs a tree per part here.
  - With gloss the picture still depends on the number of threads (1078 pixels by at most 3 of 255
    between one thread and six), before and after.
