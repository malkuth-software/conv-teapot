The Utah Teapot in CONV geometry

Every node of this teapot lies on its surface. The surface is rebuilt through the nodes by CONV, traced by correspondence transport, and simulated as a rigid body whose collision shape is taken from the nodes.

Baked mesh

Bake vertices samples the CONV surfaces once to a triangle mesh, drawn by a CPU rasterizer with no surface evaluation per frame. Bake light then stores, for every texel of a light map, the surface’s own colour and how much of the light reaches it past the teapot itself; light is computed per pixel from the normal as it is turned now, and highlights and the floor’s reflection are added each frame from emitters along the mirror direction. The bake goes with the teapot: moving it without turning it, or moving the camera, changes nothing in it, and the shadow on the floor is carried along. While the teapot turns, shadows come from the shadow map; when it rests, the lit fraction is found again. With moving reflections off, the map holds the whole picture as seen from the camera and is dropped when the teapot moves.

Simulation

The drop is a rigid-body simulation shown at half speed. Drag the teapot: it is held at the point you picked and swings from there. Drag the background, or hold Shift, to orbit. Scroll to zoom. Double-click to poke. Arrow keys fly the camera forward, back and sideways, Page Up and Page Down raise and lower it, Shift slows it, Home returns it.

The Utah Teapot was created by Martin Newell at the University of Utah; later versions are by Jim Blinn, Frank Crow and Cem Yuksel. The 1975–1987 versions here come from the original Bézier data (the flat bottom cap is an addition); the 2026 surfaces and the 1987 bottom are points sampled from the official Utah Teapot page. These are Modified Utah Teapots. GPU version · Paper (PDF) · Source Geometry: Compact Ordered Nodal Variation with ledger-free admission. Rendering runs on one CPU thread in WebAssembly; no GPU is used.
