# The GPU version

Live: https://malkuth-software.github.io/conv-teapot/conv-teapot-gpu/ (also at `/gpu/`).

The same teapot as the CPU version, drawn and simulated by WebGL 2.

| What | Where it runs | How |
|---|---|---|
| CONV compile | GPU, once per teapot | The engine hands over the lattice nodes and the shape of every cell. A shader admits the u-cells of every row from the nodes into a texture (the CPU's `compile()`). |
| CONV surface | GPU | `shaders.js`: the admitted u-cells of every lattice row are read from that texture; the v-cell through them is admitted in the shader. The derivative along u is carried through admission as the second component of a `vec2`, as the CPU's dual numbers do. |
| The mesh | GPU, once per teapot | `mesh.js`: the bounded form of the paper's layouts from the metric. The seeds are the lattice's own nodes and each lattice cell is meshed alone, to a distance (0.003) and an angle (8 degrees) by default. The surface is sampled once, eight places to a cell each way, into a texture. One fragment a cell reads its two edges and its middle line each way and finds the fewest equal steps (1 to 16) that keep a chord within the distance and the normal's turn within the angle. An edge's count depends on the edge alone, so the two cells that share it agree; the cell's inside takes the largest count each way and its border vertices are placed at the edge's own steps. The cells' triangle counts are summed on the CPU; then one fragment a triangle corner evaluates the surface there. Three texels a triangle, in order, and no vertex or index buffer. A grid of the surface's points and normals over each part is still kept, for the maps of light and of normals. |
| Normals | GPU, every pixel | The fragment shader evaluates the surface again at the pixel's (u, v). |
| Other surfaces and the texture's interpolator | GPU | The Bezier patches and the control mesh are evaluated in the shader from their control points and nodes. A texture is a lattice of colours: interpolated by CONV, bilinearly, or by the nearest node (the page's three filters). |
| Joins | GPU | A band stores where its two sides are on the body and the tube; the shader evaluates both and blends them. Trims are tested per pixel. |
| Normal at each pixel | GPU | Read from the map of the surface's normals laid over each part, between its four nearest texels; the surface can still be evaluated at every pixel (the page's Normal control). |
| The picture's shader | GPU | Compiled for the settings as they are: every setting the shader would test at each pixel is written into it as a constant, so the pixel's work holds no decision made before the frame began and none of the code for what is switched off. One is compiled the first time its settings are used (0.2 to 0.4 s) and kept. |
| Shadows | GPU | A map of distance along the light, looked up as the CPU's is, drawn again only when the teapot, its pose or the sun changes (not when the eye moves); where its texels disagree, light rays traced in the shader. |
| Rays | GPU | A tree of boxes over the mesh, written in the order a ray walks it: each node is one texel of four whole numbers (its box in 65 535ths of the teapot's box, and the node to go on from on a miss), so a step is one read, a ray keeps no stack and makes no decision but hit or miss. The tree halves a part's cells by where they are in space, down to one cell, then the cell's steps down to one pair of triangles; its layout is planned on the CPU from the cells' boxes, which the GPU finds, and the GPU fills in the boxes. Two exact shortcuts for a mirror ray: a box wholly behind the tangent plane the ray leaves is skipped; and each cell knows which blocks of its own part (sixteen to a part) lie wholly behind its tangent planes, so a ray from it skips those. For most of the body's outside that is the whole body. The mirror ray is traced one bounce, so the teapot shows itself; where it misses, emitters: sky and window above, the floor's pattern and shadow below. A polished floor shows the teapot's image, drawn from the mirrored eye. |
| Cutaway | GPU | Crossings of the kept half are counted in the stencil buffer; an odd count shows the cut face. |
| Room light | GPU | The body is drawn from 256 directions; every texel of a map over the parts looks itself up in each picture: open fraction and mean open direction. |
| Light the body throws on itself | GPU | For each direction the body is peeled into depth layers, each carrying its light; every texel takes the layer it faces. Repeated five times. Found again when the body comes to rest in a new attitude, three directions a frame, while the picture keeps the light found before. |
| Rigid body | GPU | `wrench.js`: PlaySuite's wrench-transport solver, ported to a fragment shader. One run of the shader is one frame of the solver: contact at every hull vertex within the margin of the floor, the impulses each contact retains, the passes of Newton with exact line search on the 6 x 6 system, rolling resistance, restitution, sleep. The body's state is a few texels and each contact vertex keeps its impulses in its own pair of texels: the solve also writes the twist after each of its passes, and each vertex follows its own row through the passes from those, without solving again. The state is read back each frame. (`physics.js` keeps an earlier, simpler step, selectable in the page.) |
| Picking | GPU | Part and (u, v) are drawn to a float target and one pixel is read. |
| Mass, centre of mass, inertia | GPU, once per teapot | `wrench.js`: a shader sums signed tetrahedra over the faces of the collision hulls; the solver takes its mass, centre and inertia from that. |
| Rows of the unified teapot | GPU, once per teapot | `rows.js`: the rows of the body, the lid, the handle and the spout are placed in shaders by CONV's own error, as the CPU's `plan_rows_by_error` does. The engine hands over the meridian or seam of the sampled patches. Passes over small textures find the wanted spacing at each station, the one scale that gives the asked number of rows (bisection in one texel), each row's arc length from the projective grading, each cell's distance from the source curve, and keep the plan with the smallest error over nine rounds. |
| Nodes | GPU, once per teapot | `build.js`: the nodes of the 1975, 1976 and 1987 lattices are sampled from the Bezier patches in a shader; the nodes of the unified body and lid are the meridian at each row turned about the axis; the nodes of the handle and spout are points of the source patches' CONV surfaces around each row. |
| Joins | GPU, once per teapot | `build.js`: for each column of the tube, a shader finds where it crosses the body's wall (a search along the tube, then bisection, each step a bisection on the body for the wall's radius at that height), then walks the band's two sides from there on the body and along the tube in steps that read each surface's own speed, and writes the two parameter lattices and the trims. |
| Collision hulls | GPU, once per teapot | `wrench.js`: the convex hull of each set of points, by wrapping. For every ordered pair of points one fragment turns a plane about their line until no point is outside it; if all points are then on or inside it, the two are on a face, the points on the plane are the face's corners, and the pair is a side of the face if no corner is to its right. Each side with the face's lowest-numbered corner is a triangle of a fan over the face. Every fragment has to agree on which points are on a plane, so the decisions are exact: the points are put on a grid of whole numbers (16 000 steps to the set's farthest coordinate, a few microns) and the side of a plane is the sign of a determinant worked out in 32-bit whole numbers in three parts. Points within 0.1 mm of an earlier one are welded first, as the engine's builder does. The engine's own hull builder is not run unless its simulation is used (the other solvers, or the checks). |
| Points for the collision hulls | GPU, once per teapot | `build.js`: the rings of surface points the hulls are made from, and the body's and lid's outlines the rings are chosen from, are evaluated in a shader. |
| What is left in the engine | CPU (WebAssembly), once per teapot | Bookkeeping: chaining the source patches into a meridian or seam and tabulating it, the shape of each cell (which stencil each node takes), choosing 16 outline rows for a hull, the rounding of a trim's outline, and the 2026 version's nodes, which are data. The engine also compiles its own copy of the cells, for the checks and for the point under the pointer. The engine asks the page for each piece of the work above as it builds (`BuildHooks` in `src/teapot.hpp`, the calls in `gpu/main.cpp`) and uses what comes back. Nothing on the CPU is evaluated per frame, per vertex or per pixel. |

## Measured against the CPU version

- Surface: at 12 000 points, largest distance 2.3e-6, largest angle between normals 0.027 degrees (32-bit floats against 64-bit), with the cells compiled on the GPU. The Bezier patches agree to 1.1e-6 and the control mesh to 4e-7.
- Picture at 960 x 600 against the CPU ray tracer, share of pixels differing by more than 8 of 255: porcelain 0.25%, polished floor 0.25%, texture 0.39%, chrome 0.55%, cutaway 0.96%.
- A frame, in a headless browser on the laptop used for the paper, before and after the mesh, tree, normal map, cached shadow map and compiled-in settings (2026-11-12). At 2560 x 1600 with chrome, gloss, the polished floor, traced rays, gathered room light and a 4096 shadow map: 111 ms before, 65 ms after. The same without traced rays: 45 and 15 ms. Porcelain without gloss: 16 ms after. At 960 x 600 at the page's defaults: 10 and 3.8 ms. The unified teapot is 73 560 triangles at the default mesh (132 096 on the grid before), 55 428 at the coarse setting and 135 040 at the fine one; the mesh and tree take 0.05 to 0.1 s to make.
- Where the time of a traced frame goes: a ray's step takes about a nanosecond and most mirror rays now take under 16 steps; the lid and the inside of the handle take 64 to 255, because the lid is hollow round its knob and a ray from it has the lid itself to look at. Those rays are most of the 50 ms that tracing still costs at 2560 x 1600.
- Pictures against the build before, seven views at 960 x 600, share of pixels differing by more than 8 of 255: 0.15% to 0.6%, along edges; 1.65% seen from above in chrome, where the spout now shows the body's rim and the lid's knob, which the earlier tree missed.
- Room light and gathered light against the CPU's baked renderer: mean difference 1 to 2 of 255. The two estimate the same integrals differently (32 rays a texel there, 256 directions for all texels here).
- Rigid body: the shader's solver against the CPU's, frame by frame, largest difference of the pose's position over the whole run (model units; the teapot is about 3 high): a drop of 600 frames 2.6e-3 (2026) and 3e-6 (1976); a grab at the handle dragged and released over 360 frames 8e-5; a poke 2e-5 to 4e-5. Both come to rest in the same pose to 3e-4. The shader works in 32-bit floats and stops Newton at a relative gradient of 1e-5 where the CPU stops at 1e-9. A frame of it takes 1.4 to 2.4 ms with the read-back.
- While the teapot is held, thrown and left to fall, four times over 970 frames, in a headless browser that draws the picture in 15 ms: the solver 3 ms a frame on average and 13 to 14 ms in the few frames with 80 points in contact; no frame of the picture over 25 ms while the body moves. Three things made frames of 0.4 to 1.2 s there before 2026-11-12 and are gone: the page found the light the body throws on itself again on every frame of motion; the room light was baked again each time the body turned over; and the light the body throws on itself was found in one frame when the body came to rest.
- Mass, centre of mass and inertia from the shader against the engine's: mass 8e-7 (relative), centre 2e-7 m, inertia 4e-6 (relative).
- Rows placed on the GPU against the CPU's own plan, for the error both aim at (largest distance of the CONV meridian from the source meridian, measured on the CPU in 64-bit): body 6.95e-4 against 7.03e-4, lid 2.53e-4 against 2.46e-4; at the taller height scale body 6.62e-4 against 5.79e-4, lid 2.25e-4 against 2.33e-4. Same number of rows (73 and 55). The first plan, by curvature, agrees to 2e-4 to 7e-4 in arc length; the kept plans' rows are up to 0.16 apart (0.31 at the taller scale) in arc length on meridians 8.5 long, because the search keeps one of several plans whose errors are within a few percent of each other and 32-bit arithmetic picks a different one. Placing both sheets takes about 0.2 s, once. The nodes the shader writes on those rows are within 6e-7 of the engine's on the same rows.
- The teapot built on the GPU against the engine building it alone, both on the engine's rows (the Check button does this): nodes of the sampled lattices within 4.8e-7; the joins' lattices and trims within 1.1e-5 in parameters (a row or column is 1); the body's mass from the GPU's hull points within 1e-7 (relative). The 1975, 1976 and 1987 nodes are within 4.8e-7. Building the unified teapot this way takes about 0.4 s: 4 sheets of rows, 2 tubes, 4 joins, 96 sets of hull points, 2 sheets of revolution. Handle and spout rows: 25 and 37, errors 1.1e-4 and 2.9e-4.
- The solver by number of contact points, a frame with a read-back after each pass: 1.9 ms of state pass at 20 points, 4.6 ms at 80, and 1.6 to 2.2 ms for what the vertices retain. Newton with a line search is sequential work, and a fragment does it no faster than a CPU core does (0.13 ms a frame on the CPU); the state is written through four render targets so that six fragments run it.
- Hulls made on the GPU against the engine's hull builder, all five versions: mass within 4e-6 to 3e-5 (relative), centre of mass within 7e-6 m, inertia within 5e-5 (relative); every corner of the engine's hulls is within 1e-6 m of one here. The GPU keeps a few more corners (895 against 892 for the unified teapot), where the engine merges faces within half a degree of each other and drops corners left on a straight edge. Making the hulls takes 0.07 to 0.3 s.
- A drop of 600 frames on the GPU's hulls against the CPU solver on the engine's hulls: the 1976 teapot within 1e-5 throughout; the 2026 within 6e-4; the unified teapot up to 0.07 units apart while it bounces and 0.006 at rest, where on the engine's hulls the two solvers stay within 2e-3. The few corners the engine drops are enough to change how a bounce goes.

## Not the same

- The unified teapot's body and lid have their rows where the GPU placed them, not where the CPU version places them; the two surfaces are each within the errors above of the same source.

- Traced rays meet the mesh's triangles, not the surface itself. For rays, a cell with any part shown is whole: under a join's band the part it lies on is not cut away.

## Build

`sh gpu/build.sh` (needs Emscripten) compiles the engine and assembles the page; `sh gpu/assemble.sh` reassembles it after a change to the shaders, renderer, physics or page only.
